# Approval Control Redesign

## Problem

An approval workflow contained the right control intent but required too much repetitive item-by-item comparison before execution.

The core issue was not a lack of control. It was that the reviewer lacked a concise consolidated view of the information that actually required approval.

## What I changed

I helped redesign the process around a batch-level summary that surfaced the key information before execution.

The redesigned control emphasized:

- consolidated totals instead of repeated navigation;
- clear separation between normal items and exceptions;
- a review point before execution;
- preservation of human accountability;
- a clearer audit trail for what was reviewed.

## Technology

This initiative was primarily process engineering and control redesign rather than a coding-heavy automation.

## Outcome

The redesign removed a large amount of low-value repetitive validation while keeping the approval responsibility with the reviewer.

## What this demonstrates

Automating an inefficient sequence is not always the best solution.

Sometimes the highest-value improvement is to change the control point itself: present the right information once, preserve the decision that matters, and remove the repetitive work around it.

Exact operational volumes, internal baselines, production records, and company-specific control details are intentionally omitted.
